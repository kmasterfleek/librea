# Grade 2: Everything Can Be Remembered

**Theme**: Memory as Architecture
**Core Capacity**: Encoding complex information in space, body, and structure

---

## Philosophy

Second grade is the year memory stops being accidental and becomes architectural. Children learn that the human mind can hold vast information — not through repetition and flashcards, but through *spatial encoding, physical structure, and movement*. The quipu deepens. The star compass begins. The garden becomes a memory palace.

## Key Practices

### Positional Quipu
Children learn that *where* a knot sits on the string changes its meaning. Bottom position = ones. Next position = tens. This is place value — the same concept as our number system — learned through fingers and string before it ever appears on paper.

### The 8-Direction Star Compass
Children learn to orient in space using cardinal and ordinal directions. The playground is marked. Every navigation instruction uses compass language. "The pencils are northwest of the rug." By spring, children navigate the school without maps using directional memory.

### The Garden Songline
Each class creates a walking path through the garden. At each station (a rock, a tree, a raised bed), specific knowledge lives. Children walk the path and recall the knowledge at each point. The path is walked daily. Over months, the knowledge becomes spatial — it lives *in the walk*, not in the mind alone.

## Observable Growth

By June, a second grader can:

- Create a quipu using positional notation (ones and tens)
- Navigate the school using 8-direction compass language
- Walk the garden songline and recall knowledge at each station
- Compare data across seasons ("We had more rain in winter. Is that why the lettuce grew?")
- Teach a kindergartener how to tie a basic quipu
- Participate in collective quipu reading and identify group patterns

## The Key Insight for This Year

When a child walks a songline and recalls that *this rock holds the water cycle* and *that tree holds the butterfly migration*, they are navigating a **knowledge graph** with their body. The information is not stored as text in their head. It is stored as *spatial relationships between physical locations*. This is exactly how vector databases work — information encoded as positions in space, retrieved by proximity. The child is doing with their feet what a search engine does with mathematics. And they will never forget how it feels.
