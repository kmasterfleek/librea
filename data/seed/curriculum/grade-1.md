# Grade 1: Everything Has a Pattern

**Theme**: Patterns Are Everywhere
**Core Capacity**: Recognizing, creating, and communicating repeating structures

---

## Philosophy

First graders are natural pattern detectors. This year amplifies that instinct and gives it structure. Children learn that patterns are not decoration — they are information. A pattern in the garden predicts what happens next. A pattern in a song helps you remember. A pattern in a quipu tells a story without words.

## Key Practice: The Weekly Quipu

Students maintain a **week-long quipu** — five days of observations encoded in knots and color. Each Friday, the quipu is "read" to a partner and then to the class.

**Progression across the year**:
- **Sep-Nov**: 2-3 colors, counting categories. "How many birds? How many clouds?"
- **Dec-Feb**: Add *knot types*: small knot vs. large knot = different values. Introduction of the idea that *how* you encode matters, not just *what*.
- **Mar-Jun**: Children choose their own categories. Some invent new encodings. Teacher watches for children who create systems no one taught them — this is emergent pattern literacy.

## What the Teacher Does

Surfaces patterns for the class without giving answers. "I noticed that three of you recorded more butterflies on sunny days. Does that mean something?" Lets the children debate. Records observations into ruvector.

New this year: teacher begins using **dimensional language casually**. "Marcus noticed three things about the tomato plant — its height, its color, and how many leaves. That's three ways to describe one plant." No formalization. Just planting the seed.

---

## Observable Growth

By June, a first grader can:

- Maintain a weekly quipu with 3+ categories and read it back
- Identify and extend a pattern (visual, auditory, or numerical)
- Infer a partner's observations from their encoding
- Make a prediction based on an observed pattern ("I think more ants come when it's hot")
- Create their own encoding system for a new observation type
- Write 2-3 sentences about what they noticed

---

## The Key Insight for This Year

A first grader who says "The butterflies come when the marigolds bloom, and the marigolds bloom when it's sunny" has just described a **causal chain across three variables**. They've identified a temporal pattern, connected it to a condition, and used it to predict. This is the same structure as: *attendance drops when home stability drops, and home stability drops when there's a move*. The cognitive architecture is identical. The domain doesn't matter. The pattern recognition does.
