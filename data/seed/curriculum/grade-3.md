# Grade 3: Everything Has Direction

**Theme**: Navigation as Thinking
**Core Capacity**: Orienting in space using multiple signals simultaneously

---

## Philosophy

Third grade is the year the body becomes a scientific instrument. Children move from observing the world to *navigating* it — using stars, waves, sounds, and memory rather than maps and screens. The Polynesian navigation tradition becomes central. This is not a history unit. Children learn to navigate. The cognitive payoff: holding multiple signals in mind simultaneously, making decisions under uncertainty, and trusting sensory input over external instructions.

## Key Practices

### The 16-Point Star Compass
Expanding from 8 to 16 directions. Children learn to orient anywhere on campus without visual cues. Compass roses are not on paper — they're painted on the ground and internalized through daily practice.

### Blindfolded Navigation
Pairs: one child blindfolded, one generating signals (claps, voice, movement). The blindfolded child navigates to a target using only sound and spatial memory. This develops: trust in non-visual senses, spatial updating, and the understanding that *multiple weak signals together create strong information*.

### Parachute Wave-Reading
The class holds a parachute. Subgroups create wave patterns. One child sits in the center, eyes closed, and reads the pattern through vibration. This is how Polynesian navigators read ocean swells through the hull. Children learn to sense structured information through their bodies.

### Multi-Signal Decision-Making
By spring, navigation challenges combine: compass direction + sound signal + memory of the route. Children must integrate 3+ information streams to reach a destination. This is **sensor fusion** — the same problem self-driving cars solve. Eight-year-olds solve it with their bodies.

## Observable Growth

By June, a third grader can:

- Orient using 16-point compass directions without visual aids
- Navigate a multi-step course using sound, memory, and compass together
- Read a partner's wave pattern through a parachute
- Maintain a multi-week scientific investigation with data recorded in 2+ media
- Teach a younger student one Omega skill (quipu, songline, or compass)
- Walk a songline of the school grounds and recall knowledge at 10+ stations

## The Key Insight for This Year

When a child navigates blindfolded by combining sound direction, compass memory, and step counting, they are doing something profoundly important: **making a decision from incomplete, multi-source information**. No single signal is sufficient. The sound tells direction but not distance. The compass tells orientation but not obstacles. Step counting tells distance but not direction. Only by *integrating all three simultaneously* can they navigate.

This is the same structure as: *GPA tells achievement but not engagement. Attendance tells presence but not learning. SEL tells emotional health but not academic progress. Only by looking at all dimensions together do you see the student.*

The child who has navigated blindfolded with three signals will not find "15-dimensional student vectors" strange. They already know what it feels like to think across dimensions. They just did it with their body.
