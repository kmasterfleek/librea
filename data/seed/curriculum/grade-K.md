# Kindergarten: Everything Counts

**Theme**: The Senses as Instruments
**Core Capacity**: Sorting, encoding, and reading the physical world

---

## Philosophy

Before a child can think in dimensions, they must learn that the world *can be counted, sorted, and encoded*. Kindergarten is the year children discover that their senses are measurement tools and that everything around them carries information they can capture with their hands.

No screens. No devices. String, seeds, soil, songs.

| Time | Block | What Happens |

| 8:00-8:30 | Garden Morning | Arrive, tend personal micro-garden plot. Water, observe, touch soil. Silent observation for first 5 minutes. |

| 11:00-11:30 | Number World | Counting, grouping, simple arithmetic — always with physical manipulatives, never worksheets first. |
| 11:30-12:15 | Lunch + Recess | |
| 12:15-1:00 | Garden Afternoon | Focused garden task: planting, measuring, harvesting. Record one observation. |
| 1:00-1:45 | Partner Decoding | Pair work: read your partner's quipu or sorting. "What did they notice that you missed?" |
| 1:45-2:15 | Song & Movement | Songs that encode sequences, patterns, directions. Movement games that build spatial awareness. |
| 2:15-2:30 | Closing Circle | One sentence from each child: "Today I noticed..." |

## The Quipu Practice (Core)

Children begin with **one-color quipu**: a single string where knots represent counts.

**Month 1-2**: Count one thing per day from the garden. Tie one knot per item observed. "I saw three butterflies" = three knots.

**Month 3-4**: Add a second color. Red string = animals. Yellow string = plants. Now two parallel data streams, one child.

**Month 5-6**: A third color. Blue = weather. Children are now encoding multi-category observations in a physical medium every day.

**Month 7-9**: Partner reading. Can you look at someone else's quipu and tell what they observed? Friday ritual: hang all quipus on a shared line. Stand back. What do we see together?

Children are building the cognitive substrate of dimensional encoding — multiple attributes describing the same observations — with their hands.

## How Traditional Subjects Map

| Standard Subject | Omega Kindergarten |
|------------------|--------------------|
| Math | Counting, grouping, sorting with physical objects. Quipu knots = quantity. Position on string = category. |
| ELA | Daily story, vocabulary building from observations, letter formation. Closing circle oral language practice. |
| Science | Garden: plant life cycles, weather observation, animal identification. Soil as living system. |
| Social Studies | Partner decoding (perspective-taking), community circle, shared garden responsibility. |
| Art | Quipu as aesthetic object, color theory through string selection, observational drawing. |
| PE/Movement | Spatial awareness games, direction games (left/right/near/far), rhythm and pattern songs. |

## What the Teacher Does

**In front of students**: Facilitates, asks questions, models curiosity. "What do you think this means?" Never delivers answers first. Creates space for children to notice before being told.

**Behind the scenes**: Captures observations into ruvector device using voice notes. Over time, the system surfaces patterns:
- Which children encode most naturally through knots? Through drawing? Through song?
- Who consistently notices things others miss?
- Where are the struggles that look like disengagement but are actually processing?

The technology is invisible to children. It serves teacher perception.

---

## Observable Growth (Not Graded, Not Tested)

By June, a kindergartener in this program can:

- Tie a multi-color quipu encoding 3+ categories of observation
- Read a partner's quipu and describe what they observed
- Sit in the garden for 5 minutes in focused observation
- Name something they noticed that surprised them
- Participate in the Friday collective pattern-recognition ritual
- Tend their garden plot with increasing independence

## Materials

- Cotton string in 6+ colors (bulk, cheap)
- Wooden dowels or branches for quipu hanging
- Garden plots (one per child, 2x2 feet minimum)
- Seeds, soil, basic garden tools
- Picture books featuring patterns, nature, counting
- No screens, no tablets, no apps

---

## The Key Insight for This Year

A five-year-old who ties three knots on a red string and two knots on a yellow string has just created a **two-dimensional data point**. They don't know that. They don't need to know that. But the cognitive pathway — *one thing can be described by multiple measurements simultaneously* — is being laid down in their hands and their memory.

This is the foundation everything else builds on.
